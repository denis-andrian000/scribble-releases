Privacy policy

Effective 1 November 2026

Scribble is an app for Windows, Android and web browsers, made by Omniz. It has no server of its own and it does not collect, sell or share your data. This page explains what Scribble stores, where, and what it sends to the services you choose to connect.

What stays on your device

  • Your notebook: notes, tasks and projects are ordinary Markdown files in a folder you choose (by default Documents/Scribble Notebook).
  • App data: your settings, the look of your buddy, chat history and simple usage counts (number of requests and tokens) are kept in Scribble's own folder on your computer.
  • API keys: the Gemini or Claude key you add is stored encrypted using your operating system's secure storage on Windows. On Android it is kept in Scribble's private app storage, which other apps can't read.
  • In a browser (the web version), the notebook, chats, settings and API key are kept in the browser's own storage for this site (IndexedDB and local storage) on your device. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes them from that device; your Google Drive copy stays.
  • On Android, the notebook, chats and settings live in Scribble's private app storage (and in your Google Drive, see below).

Android permissions

  • Display over other apps: only if you turn on the floating buddy, so it can be drawn over other apps.
  • Notifications: for reminders (morning brief, due tasks, recap) and the small notice Android requires while the buddy is floating.
  • Your Google account: Android's own account picker is used to sign in; Scribble never sees your password.

What is sent to your AI provider

When you chat, Scribble sends your message, any files you attach, and the parts of your notebook the assistant needs to answer to the AI provider you connected:

  • Google Gemini (Google LLC), under Google's Gemini API terms. On Google's free tier, Google may use prompts and responses to improve its products, and they may be read by human reviewers. Don't send passwords or confidential information while using the free tier.
  • Claude (Anthropic PBC), under Anthropic's commercial terms and privacy policy.

The request goes directly from your device to that provider, using your own key. Scribble's developer never receives it.

Signing in with Google

If you choose to sign in with Google, Scribble asks for your basic profile (name, email address and picture) and access to a hidden app-data folder in your own Google Drive (drive.appdata). Scribble uses this only to:

  • show which account you're signed in with, and
  • keep a copy of your Scribble data (notebook, tasks, chats and settings) in that hidden folder, so it follows you to your other computers and your phone or tablet.

In a browser, sign-in uses Google's own sign-in window (Google Identity Services). Google gives Scribble a short-lived access token (about an hour), kept in the browser's storage on your device; no password or long-lived token is stored.

Scribble cannot see or change any other files in your Drive. Your data goes directly between your device and Google; it is not sent to Scribble's developer or anyone else.

Scribble's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Update checks

Scribble asks GitHub, where its releases are published, whether a newer version exists, and downloads updates from there. The web version is served by GitHub Pages and updates itself from there. Like any website, GitHub sees your IP address when this happens; no other information is sent.

What Scribble does not do

  • No analytics, tracking or advertising.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.
  • No use of your data to train AI models by Scribble.

Deleting your data

  • Delete your notebook folder, and uninstall Scribble to remove its app data from your computer. On Android, uninstalling Scribble removes everything it stored on the device.
  • To remove the copy in Google Drive: in Google Drive, open Settings → Manage apps → Scribble → Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • To stop Scribble's access to your Google account, go to myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove Scribble.

Who Scribble is for

Scribble is meant for people aged 18 or older, in line with the terms of the AI providers it connects to.

Visit counting

This website and the browser version of Scribble count visits with GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly service: no cookies, no tracking across sites, and no personal information. It records only the page visited, the site you came from, your screen size and a rough location from your IP address (the address itself is not stored), so we can see how many people use Scribble. The Windows and Android apps don't use it.

Changes
